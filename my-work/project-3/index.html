01 · OVERVIEW

LumiTrack

A teaching dashboard that gives design faculty their time back, so mentoring is about the student, not the spreadsheet.

Grading design is subjective, feedback is qualitative and progress isn't a straight line. Yet the faculty at our college were running all of it on tools built for something else. LumiTrack is a learning management system made for design colleges, starting with the people who teach in them.

02 · WHERE IT STARTED

It started with a group project where we all got the same marks

Since our second semester, almost everything we've made as UX students has been group work. In nearly every group, someone didn't pull their weight, and everyone still walked away with the same grade. It never felt fair.

So we looked at how our college handles feedback. Classroom for submissions, Excel for grading, WhatsApp for mentoring, Teams for schedules, TCS iON for attendance. So many platforms, and still,

Feedback is inconsistent, expectations are unclear, guidance is rarely personal, group work hides individual effort, and faculty are drowning in admin.

so we went and asked

07 · THE DESIGN

A week with LumiTrack

A prettier LMS wouldn't fix this. So follow one faculty member through her week, from the morning dashboard to the attendance register.

View the prototype →

01 · HOME

Today's classes, schedule and what needs attention, on one screen.

Why? Faculty were piecing their day together from Teams, Excel and WhatsApp.

Hover or tap the numbers.

02 · THE ASSIGNMENT

Deliverables and evaluation criteria sit on the assignment itself.

Why? Criteria were unclear, or invisible to students, until the marks came back.

03 · REVIEWING A GROUP'S WORK

Draw on the work, keep the rubric beside it, and see who did what.

Why? “Group work hides individual contribution.”

04 · THE COMMENT YOU'D TYPE TWENTY TIMES

LumiTrack notices the same feedback across groups and offers to send it once.

Why? “Why do I have to repeat the same thing 20 times?”

05 · A STUDENT, ACROSS SEMESTERS

A face, attendance, a progress graph and every project, semester by semester.

Why? With so many students, faculty couldn't match work to the person who had asked about it.

06 · ATTENDANCE

Mark everyone at once, link TCS iON or Sheets, or snap the paper register.

Why? Attendance was a daily chore, entered twice.

Every screen answers something we heard

We started from five what ifs. This is where each one ended up.

  1. What if faculty could see progress at a glance?→01 Home, 05 Student profile
  2. What if process mattered as much as output?→03 Reviewing a group's work
  3. What if submission, feedback and review lived in one place?→03 Reviewing, 04 The repeated comment
  4. What if faculty had a helper?→04 The repeated comment, 06 Attendance
  5. What if grading was transparent?→02 The assignment, 03 The rubric

08 · WHAT FACULTY SAID

We put it in front of faculty again

Testing session · faculty

On simplicity

TCS iON is very complex. This is not complex at all, it's very easy to use.

On visibility

Solves a major Google Classroom problem. Right now, we don't know if people are even opening the classroom unless they submit something.

On time

It is at least streamlining everything into one place. This is good, it saves time.

09 · WHAT WE'D TEST NEXT

Where this stands

A four week student project, tested with faculty but not built. Three things we'd want to know before it is.

01

Does AI suggested feedback stay personal?

Watch whether faculty edit the suggestions, or start accepting them as they are.

02

Is the contribution log honest?

Students log their own parts. Do their teammates agree with what's logged?

03

Would faculty trust a photo of the register?

Count how often photo attendance needs fixing by hand.

10 · FUTURE SCOPE

Today a focused LMS. Tomorrow the whole institute.

LumiTrack starts with feedback, evaluation and mentoring. It could grow into an education resource management system for a whole institute, a modern alternative to tools like TCS iON.

01

Admin and accreditation

NAAC documentation and analytics for academic quality.

02

Faculty workload

Time allocation, payroll, HR and leave.

03

Campus operations

Event approvals, room booking and notices.

04

One record per student

Attendance, grades, work and portfolios, feeding curriculum planning.

We built it with design colleges in mind, and we'd like to take it to other courses next.
