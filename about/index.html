NAMASTE, I'M ANUBHUTI

I grew up on the southern banks of the Ganga, in a city where stories live in street markets, political posters weathered by monsoons and hand painted shop signs. I didn't have a word for it then, but those were my first lessons in design: visuals as conversations between people, culture and time.

Most of my work starts with research: talking to people, reading everything I can find, and only then opening Figma. When I'm not designing, I'm usually out with a camera, trying a new art technique, or planning the next trip.

Solving a problem is only half the work. The other half is making sure the solution resonates with people and earns their engagement. UX tells me what people need. Storytelling helps me build the narrative around the solution, so it stays with people long after they've used it.

HOW I WORK

Start with people, not screens.

I talk to the people I'm designing for and read everything I can find: interviews, sitting in with real users, studies and papers.

Find the one problem worth solving.

I sort what I hear into patterns with affinity mapping and thematic analysis, then write the problem down in one sentence. Most of my time goes here, because a clear problem makes everything after it easier.

Sketch, then flows, then screens.

I move from rough sketches to flows to Figma. When timing or motion matters, I prototype in code so it can be felt instead of imagined.

Check it with people, then tell its story.

I test with real users and change what doesn't work. Then I shape the story around the solution, so the people who use it and the people who build it both understand why it matters.
